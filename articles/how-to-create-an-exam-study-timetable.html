How to Create an Exam Study Timetable That Works

Last updated: 19 November 2026

A timetable turns “I must study” into “on Tuesday at 4 pm I will do Mathematics for 40 minutes”. It removes the daily decision about what to do next and makes sure no subject gets forgotten. Here is how to build one you can actually follow.

Step 1: Collect your dates

Write down every exam date and time. Count how many days are left before each one. Subjects with an earlier exam need to be revised first.

Step 2: List topics and rate them

For each subject, list the topics and rate each one: confident, unsure or lost. The “unsure” and “lost” topics should get the most time.

Step 3: Decide how much time you really have

Look at a normal week. Subtract school, travel, meals, chores, sport and rest. What is left is your study time. Be honest: a timetable with six hours a day that you ignore is worse than one with two hours a day that you keep.

Step 4: Break time into short sessions

Use sessions of about 30 to 45 minutes with a 10-minute break between them. One session should have one clear goal, such as “do ten practice questions on fractions”.

Step 5: Spread and mix your subjects

Do not study the same subject for the whole afternoon. Rotate subjects across the week so you return to each one several times, which helps your memory (see spaced repetition). Put your hardest subject early in the session or at the time of day when you concentrate best.

Step 6: Plan rest and a catch-up slot

Keep at least one lighter day or half-day each week, and leave one slot free for tasks that took longer than planned. Without this buffer, one bad day can break the whole plan.

Step 7: Plan revision rounds

Schedule a first pass through each subject, then a second, shorter pass, and in the last days a quick pass of summaries and mistakes only.

Tips to stick to it

  • Put the timetable where you will see it, such as above your desk or as your phone wallpaper.
  • Tick off each session when you finish it.
  • Review the plan every Sunday and adjust it.
  • If you miss a session, move it, do not drop it.
  • Turn your phone to silent during sessions.
