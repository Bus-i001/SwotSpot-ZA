How to Use Spaced Repetition

Last updated: 19 November 2026

Spaced repetition means reviewing information several times, with longer and longer gaps between each review. It is one of the most reliable ways to make facts stay in your memory, and you can start with nothing more than a pen, paper and a calendar.

Why spacing works

We forget new information quickly unless we go back to it. Each time you review something just as you are starting to forget it, your memory of it gets stronger and lasts longer. Cramming everything into one night can help for a day or two, but most of it fades soon after. Spreading the same study time over several days gives much longer-lasting results.

A simple schedule to start with

There is no single perfect timetable, but this pattern is a good starting point:

  1. Study the topic for the first time.
  2. Review it the next day.
  3. Review it again three days later.
  4. Review it after about a week.
  5. Review it after about two weeks, and again before the test.

Each review can be short, around ten minutes, and should use active recall rather than rereading.

Plan it backwards from your test

Suppose your test is in three weeks. Learn a topic in week one, review it twice in week two and once more in week three. Write the review dates in your planner or phone calendar when you first study each topic, so you do not have to remember to remember.

The Leitner box method with paper flashcards

This is an easy way to space your reviews without a calendar. You need flashcards and three boxes or envelopes.

  1. Write a question on the front of each card and the answer on the back. Put every card in Box 1.
  2. Go through Box 1 every day. If you get a card right, move it to Box 2. If you get it wrong, it stays in Box 1.
  3. Go through Box 2 every few days. A correct card moves to Box 3. A wrong one goes back to Box 1.
  4. Go through Box 3 about once a week. A wrong card goes back to Box 1.

Cards you find hard come up often, and cards you know well come up rarely, so your time goes where it is needed.

Apps

Flashcard apps can schedule the reviews for you. Anki is a well-known free flashcard tool, though availability and cost can differ between phones, so check before you download. Paper cards work just as well if you prefer them.

Tips for good flashcards

  • Put one idea on each card.
  • Use your own words and short answers.
  • Write questions, not just terms, for example “What does a ratio scale of 1 : 50 000 mean?”.
  • Add a small example or drawing when it helps.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Making cards but never reviewing them.
  • Reviewing in a big batch the night before.
  • Only reviewing the cards you already know.
  • Making cards too long.

Spaced repetition works best together with the other methods in How to study effectively.
