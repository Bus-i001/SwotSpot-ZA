How to Prepare for an Exam: A Step-by-Step Guide

Last updated: 19 November 2026

Good exam results usually come from steady preparation, not last-minute panic. This guide takes you from the first day of revision to the moment you walk into the exam room.

Step 1: Find out exactly what will be tested

Ask your teacher or check your class notes for the topics, the date, the time and how long each paper is. Find out what kinds of questions to expect, such as multiple choice, short answers or longer written answers. Write all of this down in one place.

Step 2: Start early

Begin at least two weeks before a big exam if you can, and earlier if you have several exams close together. Revising a little each day is far more effective than long sessions the night before.

Step 3: Make a topic checklist

List every topic for each subject. Next to each one, mark whether you know it well, partly, or not at all. Start with the “not at all” and “partly” topics, because that is where the most marks are waiting. You can plan the days with our study timetable generator, and read how to create an exam study timetable.

Step 4: Revise actively

Close your notes and test yourself instead of only rereading. Use active recall, and come back to each topic several times using spaced repetition. Short summaries and mind maps are good for quick revision, and you can find free examples in our study resources.

Step 5: Practise under exam conditions

Once you know the content, do practice questions, worksheets or practice papers from your teacher without looking at your notes. Set a timer that matches the real exam. Afterwards, mark your work with the memo and note what went wrong: was it missing knowledge, a careless mistake, or running out of time?

Step 6: Fix your weak spots

Use your marked work to decide what to study next. Go back to the topics where you lost marks, study them again, and test yourself again a few days later.

Step 7: The last week

  • Revise from short summaries, mind maps and your list of mistakes.
  • Do a few timed practice questions each day.
  • Prepare what you need: pens, pencils, a ruler, a calculator with working batteries, and your exam number or ID if required.
  • Check the exam timetable again so you do not miss a paper.

Step 8: The night before

Do a light review of your summaries, then stop. Pack your bag, eat a proper meal and go to bed at a reasonable time. Sleep helps your memory more than an extra hour of cramming.

Step 9: On the day

  • Eat breakfast and drink water.
  • Arrive early so you are not rushed.
  • Read the instructions and every question carefully.
  • Start with the questions you are confident about, and keep an eye on the time.
  • If you finish early, check your answers.

A quick checklist

  1. Know the topics and dates.
  2. Start two weeks ahead.
  3. Make a checklist and a timetable.
  4. Revise actively and in spaced sessions.
  5. Practise with a timer and mark your work.
  6. Rest well before the exam.
